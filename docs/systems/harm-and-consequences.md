# Harm And Consequences

Harm is one consequence type, not the entire consequence system. Sanguine Archipelago should treat damage, fear, exhaustion, social exposure, obligation, corruption, and lost access as part of the same pressure economy.

## Consequence Categories

Use consequences that fit the fiction:

- `Physical`: wounds, blood loss, poison, broken equipment, fatigue.
- `Positional`: separated, pinned, exposed, cornered, overboard.
- `Social`: embarrassment, suspicion, debt, loss of face, public accusation.
- `Institutional`: permits revoked, checkpoints raised, sanctuary denied.
- `Spiritual`: corruption, haunting, omen debt, ritual backlash.
- `Operational`: time lost, supplies spent, alarm clocks advanced.

## Harm Should Have Texture

Prefer specific harm language over abstract damage when useful.

Examples:

- "cut across the ribs"
- "saltwater in the lungs"
- "burned hand from relic backlash"
- "publicly named as a Yuma collaborator"
- "marked by the shrine's unanswered debt"

Texture helps later mechanics know what kind of pressure exists.

## Consequence Severity

Use severity based on stakes, position, and prior warnings.

- `Minor`: complicates the next action or costs a small resource.
- `Moderate`: changes position, marks harm, advances a clock, or creates a debt.
- `Severe`: removes a route, inflicts serious harm, triggers faction action, or exposes corruption.
- `Critical`: transforms the scene or campaign situation.

Severity should be telegraphed when possible. Players should feel they are managing risk, not being surprised by punishment.

## Success With Cost

Success can still hurt.

Example:

A character succeeds on `Patch` to seal a leaking engine room during battle. The ship survives, but the repair burns rare components and the smoke advances the crew fatigue clock.

The player accomplished the intent. The world still pushed back.

## Non-Harm Consequence Examples

- A `Guile` failure convinces the target but alerts an Underworld observer.
- A `Navigation` partial success reaches the island after the storm clock advances.
- A `Ward` success protects the shrine but leaves the caster spiritually visible.
- A `Broker` failure obtains the permit at the cost of Guild debt.
- A `Suppress` success controls a lane but spends ammunition and reveals position.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- every failed combat roll becoming only damage
- every social failure becoming only disadvantage
- consequences that do not change future choices
- severe consequences with no warning
- harm that ignores faction, position, or environment
