# Social Conflict

Social conflict is not a charm spell, a persuasion button, or a way to erase NPC agency. It is a structured exchange of pressure, leverage, reputation, identity, and consequence.

## Core Principle

Social rolls change position, access, commitments, and risk. They do not overwrite what a character values.

A successful social action may:

- win time
- secure a concession
- reveal a fear
- create doubt
- gain access
- shift a crowd
- isolate a rival
- bind a promise

It should not make an enemy abandon core ideology without sufficient fictional leverage.

## Social Core Checks

- `Grace`: trust, diplomacy, poise, and gentle authority.
- `Guile`: misdirection, concealment, and layered speech.
- `Pressure`: intimidation, threat, and hard authority.
- `Conviction`: oath, zeal, sacred force, and moral insistence.
- `Flourish`: spectacle, performance, seduction, and ceremonial display.
- `Presence`: attention, command, and room control.
- `Composure`: resisting bait, scandal, fear, and interrogation.

## Leverage

Leverage is what makes a demand possible.

Examples:

- proof of corruption
- faction backing
- a hostage route without cruelty
- debt owed by a guild factor
- public sympathy
- ritual authority
- control of supplies
- a secret the target cannot ignore

Without leverage, a roll may still change tone or reveal information, but it should not force a major concession.

## NPC Stance

Track stance in fiction rather than as a universal number.

Useful stance words:

- open
- cautious
- suspicious
- pressured
- cornered
- indebted
- offended
- impressed
- afraid
- committed

Stance should guide consequences and next options.

## Example: Noble Court Accusation

Intent: expose a Tetlanco noble's smuggling deal in public.

Possible approaches:

- `Presence`: seize the room's attention before guards intervene.
- `Guile`: lead the noble into contradicting their own account.
- `Conviction`: make the accusation as an oath before witnesses.
- `Composure`: withstand counter-accusation without losing face.
- `Broker`: prove the trade route through records and permits.

Specialties:

- `Read` can identify who in the room already suspects the truth.
- `Perform` can stage the reveal as public theater.
- `Bargain` can secure testimony from a reluctant clerk.

Consequences:

- Noble Courts reputation may fall even on success.
- Rebel Movements may rise if the reveal harms imperial logistics.
- Underworld Networks may mark the character as dangerous.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- mind-control outcomes from ordinary social checks
- persuasion as a universal solution
- social failure that only says "they say no"
- ignoring reputation context
- making all factions respond identically to the same argument
