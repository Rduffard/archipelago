# Clocks And Pressure

Clocks track pressure that grows over time. They make consequence visible without turning every threat into immediate harm.

## Purpose

Use clocks when a danger is:

- accumulating
- observable
- affected by player action
- too important to resolve with one roll
- useful for pacing a scene, journey, or faction response

Clocks should create urgency and choices, not hidden punishment.

## Common Clock Types

- `Alarm`: guards mobilize, a crew wakes, patrols converge.
- `Storm`: weather worsens, routes close, ships scatter.
- `Corruption`: relic pressure deepens, spirits notice, symptoms spread.
- `Faction`: reputation shifts, warrants spread, allies lose patience.
- `Scarcity`: supplies run low, ammunition dwindles, hull strain mounts.
- `Ritual`: a spell, oath, curse, or binding nears completion.
- `Social`: crowd mood turns, negotiations collapse, scandal becomes public.

## Clock Size

Use size to communicate how much room the table has.

- `4 segments`: immediate scene pressure.
- `6 segments`: complex encounter or short operation.
- `8 segments`: journey, investigation, faction response, or major hazard.
- `10+ segments`: campaign-scale transformation.

## Advancing Clocks

Advance a clock when:

- a roll fails under relevant pressure
- a success has an operational cost
- players spend too much time
- a faction acts offscreen
- a relic or environment escalates
- a chosen shortcut creates exposure

Do not advance clocks randomly if the fiction does not justify it.

## Reducing Or Freezing Clocks

Characters can interact with clocks through preparation and mastery.

Examples:

- `Signal` delays an alarm clock by spoofing patrol orders.
- `Ward` freezes a corruption clock for a scene.
- `Bargain` reduces a social collapse clock by conceding a future favor.
- `Navigation` bypasses two segments of a storm route clock.
- `Suppress` prevents an enemy advance clock from ticking this exchange.

## Example: Imperial Checkpoint

Clock: `Papers Exposed` with 6 segments.

Pressure sources:

- forged seal inspected
- nervous ally speaks out of turn
- patrol captain recognizes a ship name
- delay draws a Lilin observer

Player routes:

- `Broker`: produce the right permit.
- `Grace`: make the inspection feel routine.
- `Guile`: redirect attention toward another vessel.
- `Signal`: intercept and alter the confirmation request.

When the clock fills, the checkpoint does not simply "fail." It creates a new state: pursuit, detention, bribery, public accusation, or forced alliance.
