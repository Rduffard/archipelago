# Corruption System

Corruption is a risk/reward pressure system for forbidden power, spiritual contamination, and dangerous relic interaction.

## Design Role

Corruption should create tempting choices:

- solve a problem now
- accept a cost later
- become more legible to hostile powers
- change how factions and spirits perceive the character

Corruption is most interesting when it is useful. If corruption is only punishment, players will avoid it until forced. If it solves real problems at a visible cost, it becomes a campaign engine.

## Relationship To Core Checks

`Corruption` is a Core Check because resisting, containing, or deliberately channeling tainted power is a broad capability.

Specialties may modify how corruption is perceived, negotiated, or redirected. They should not erase the risk.

## Relationship To Specialties

Arcane Specialties such as `Attune` and `Sift` can interact with corruption by:

- identifying tainted resonance
- previewing spiritual consequence
- finding safer ritual approaches
- converting unknown danger into known danger

They should not make corruption harmless.

## Corruption Sources

Common sources:

- bleeding relics
- broken wards
- spirit bargains
- forbidden engines
- corpse-bound rituals
- storm memories
- oath violations
- repeated exposure to haunted sites

Not every arcane scene should involve corruption. Reserve it for power that changes the relationship between the character and the unseen world.

## Corruption Consequences

Possible consequences:

- a faction detects the character's exposure
- a spirit recognizes the character later
- a Core Check becomes riskier in a specific context
- dreams or omens become intrusive
- an NPC refuses physical contact or ritual proximity
- a relic becomes easier to use and harder to put down
- a corruption clock advances

Avoid consequences that only subtract agency. The best corruption consequences create new temptations.

## Relic Interaction

Relics should have personality, history, or institutional meaning. They are not generic magic items.

When introducing a relic, define:

- what it wants or remembers
- who claims it
- what it makes easier
- what it makes costly
- which factions know its name
- what corruption risk follows repeated use

Example:

A stormglass compass can reveal a safe route through impossible weather. Each use also lets the storm remember the ship's silhouette, advancing a future pursuit clock.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- corruption as random body horror with no system meaning
- relics as generic loot upgrades
- removing all corruption risk through one Specialty
- making forbidden power useless
- treating spiritual consequence as separate from faction consequence
