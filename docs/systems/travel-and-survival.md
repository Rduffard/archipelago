# Travel And Survival

Travel in Sanguine Archipelago should feel like movement through contested waters, unstable weather, local memory, faction borders, and spiritual pressure. It is not empty distance between scenes.

## Travel Questions

A journey should answer at least one:

- What route do the characters choose, and who controls it?
- What do they risk by arriving late, visible, or depleted?
- Which factions can see or tax the route?
- What does the sea, storm, reef, jungle, or ruin demand?
- What local knowledge changes the trip?

## Core Checks

Common travel checks:

- `Navigation`: route, currents, charts, arrival timing.
- `Survival`: exposure, shelter, supplies, weathering deprivation.
- `Scouting`: advance movement, hidden approaches, enemy movement.
- `Sense`: weather signs, traps, patterns, wrongness.
- `Nerve`: fear, disaster response, and action under dread.
- `Adaptation`: changing plans when the route collapses.
- `Signal`: coordination across ships, lookouts, and coded warnings.
- `Patch`: keeping hulls, engines, or bodies functioning.

## Travel Structure

Use travel as a series of pressure decisions:

1. Choose route.
2. Establish known hazards.
3. Reveal one local or faction pressure.
4. Resolve key uncertainty.
5. Apply cost, discovery, or opportunity.
6. Arrive changed.

The goal is not to roll for every mile. The goal is to make route choice matter.

## Example Routes

### Fast Imperial Channel

- Benefit: arrival before the auction begins.
- Risk: Yuma patrols, permit checks, visible ship registry.
- Likely checks: `Navigation`, `Broker`, `Signal`, `Composure`.

### Reef Shadow Passage

- Benefit: hidden approach and pirate route.
- Risk: coralborn claims, hull damage, strange tides.
- Likely checks: `Sense`, `Patch`, `Scouting`, `Survival`.

### Haunted Storm Line

- Benefit: avoids all living patrols.
- Risk: omen pressure, relic resonance, crew fear.
- Likely checks: `Focus`, `Omen`, `Ward`, `Nerve`.

## Supplies And Scarcity

Scarcity should pressure decisions without becoming accounting busywork.

Track meaningful resources:

- safe water
- repair stock
- ammunition
- ritual materials
- crew morale
- medical supplies
- favors owed at destination

Use clocks when depletion matters more than exact counts.

## Arrival States

Arrival should reflect travel choices:

- early but visible
- hidden but exhausted
- late with new information
- intact but indebted
- safe but spiritually marked
- damaged but politically unnoticed

Good travel creates new scene texture rather than simply delaying the next encounter.
