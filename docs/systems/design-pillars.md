# Design Pillars

Sanguine Archipelago is a fiction-first tabletop system about pressure, belonging, consequence, and mastery. Its rules should make the world feel political, cultural, dangerous, and alive rather than treating the setting as decoration around combat math.

## Pillar 1: Fiction Before Interface

The fiction establishes the action before the mechanic is chosen.

Ask:

1. What is the character trying to change?
2. What stands in the way?
3. What could go wrong?
4. Which Core Check best represents the pressure?
5. Which Specialty permissions apply?

Do not begin from a list of buttons or optimized actions. The sheet should support the player's declared intent, not replace it.

## Pillar 2: Broad Competency, Narrow Mastery

Core Checks keep characters broadly capable. Specialties make them distinct.

Example:

- `Precision` lets many characters aim, sabotage, or perform exact work.
- `Shoot` makes one character a practiced marksman with specific permissions.
- `Suppress` makes another character control space and enemy movement.

Both may roll Precision. They should not feel identical in play.

## Pillar 3: Permissions Over Inflation

Progression should usually unlock something a character can now attempt, access, resist, reveal, or command.

Strong rewards:

- use `Guard` instead of `Reflex` against forced movement
- gain access to a faction trainer
- ask one binding question of a relic
- reduce the social cost of entering a hostile institution
- convert an unknown hazard into a known consequence

Weak rewards:

- `+1 to all social checks`
- permanent generic attack bonuses
- rewards that ignore faction, fiction, or risk

## Pillar 4: Factions Create Pressure

Factions are not stores. They are institutions with memory, doctrine, obligations, and enemies.

A faction reward should imply a relationship:

- Who trained you?
- What did they teach you not to do?
- Who distrusts you because of it?
- What access do you now have?
- What debt or expectation follows?

The world is the morality system. Reputation records how power blocs respond to what the characters have done.

## Pillar 5: Consequences Should Move The Scene

A failed or costly roll should change something visible:

- position worsens
- a clock advances
- someone takes harm
- a faction notices
- resources burn
- access closes
- corruption marks the scene
- an NPC changes stance

Avoid empty failure. If nothing changes, the roll probably was not worth making.

## Pillar 6: Culture Is Mechanical

Yuma doctrine, Lilin ritual authority, Free Captain autonomy, Guild craft access, Faith discipline, and Sea Peoples memory should create different routes through problems.

This does not mean each faction grants a simple stat package. It means each faction teaches different permissions, imposes different obligations, and makes different choices legible.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- generic fantasy role labels that erase Archipelago culture
- optimization-only rewards
- loot treadmill assumptions
- interchangeable factions
- Specialties that are only math bonuses
- mechanics detached from consequence
- rolls where failure has no cost
- morality meters that replace faction response
