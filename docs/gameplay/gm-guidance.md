# GM Guidance

The GM's job is to make pressure legible, consequences meaningful, and character identity useful. The system works best when the table can see why a risk exists and why a particular character's training matters.

## Calling For Rolls

Call for a roll when all three are true:

- the character's intent could meaningfully succeed
- the outcome is uncertain
- failure or cost would change the scene

Do not roll to see whether competent characters can perform routine acts under no pressure.

## Naming Stakes

Before important rolls, name at least one possible cost:

- "If this goes badly, the alarm clock advances."
- "You can force the door, but failure damages the cargo inside."
- "A public threat here will work, but the Noble Courts will hear about it."
- "The relic can answer, but it may remember your voice."

Clear stakes make hard choices feel earned.

## Choosing Checks

Let approach determine the Core Check.

Example: entering a restricted archive.

- `Broker`: secure permission.
- `Guile`: mislead the clerk.
- `Breach`: open a sealed route.
- `Signal`: spoof a clearance message.
- `Presence`: walk in like authority belongs to you.

Reward creative framing without letting one check solve every situation.

## Using Specialties

Invite players to explain how a Specialty changes their action. Specialties are permissions and mastery expressions, so the player should be able to describe what their training looks like.

Good prompt:

> "You have `Brace II`. What does absorbing that impact look like here?"

Poor prompt:

> "Add your skill bonus."

## Faction Consequence

When a faction is relevant, ask:

- Who benefits from this action?
- Who is embarrassed, threatened, or exposed?
- Who has the institutional ability to respond?
- Does anyone now owe the characters access, protection, or reprisal?

Do not force every scene into faction bookkeeping. Use faction response when institutions would reasonably care.

## Failure

Failure should not halt the story unless the halt itself is the new situation.

Instead of:

- "You fail to find the route."

Prefer:

- "You find a route, but it emerges under a Yuma watch lantern."
- "You lose half a day and the storm clock advances."
- "You reach the island, but the reef has marked the hull."

## Table Tone

Keep language grounded in the Archipelago:

- docks, permits, shrines, reefs, storms, hulls, courts, oaths, patrols, spirits
- faction schools, ritual obligations, smuggler routes, guild seals, coral claims

Avoid drifting into generic dungeon language unless the specific scene supports it.
