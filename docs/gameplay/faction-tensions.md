# Faction Tensions

Faction tension is the campaign's moral and political engine. The game should not need an explicit morality meter because institutions, communities, and spirits respond to what the characters do.

## Tension Principles

- Every major faction should want something real.
- No major faction should be purely a shop interface.
- A faction's training should imply doctrine.
- A faction's help should create visibility.
- A faction's enemy should notice repeated alignment.

## Example Tensions

### Yuma Republic vs Rebel Movements

Yuma values order, industrial state power, command, and strategic control. Rebels value liberation, sabotage, local autonomy, and anti-occupation resistance.

Shared objective: stop a corrupt noble from selling weapons.

Conflict:

- Yuma wants seizure, registration, and public authority.
- Rebels want exposure, redistribution, and symbolic humiliation.

The party can satisfy both only with careful sequencing and meaningful compromise.

### Lilin Empire vs Guild Consortium

Lilin authority treats spiritual force as relationship, oath, and ritual consequence. Guild power treats dangerous systems as craft, access, and managed expertise.

Shared objective: contain a relic engine.

Conflict:

- Lilin wants ritual quarantine and spiritual accounting.
- Guild wants cataloging, controlled experimentation, and proprietary salvage.

The relic becomes a political object, not just treasure.

### Free Captains vs Noble Courts

Free Captains value autonomy, reputation, and refusal of imperial order. Noble Courts value legitimacy, inheritance, and public form.

Shared objective: remove a violent admiral.

Conflict:

- Free Captains want public defeat at sea.
- Noble Courts want a lawful transition that preserves hierarchy.

Both can oppose tyranny while disagreeing about what replaces it.

## GM Use

When preparing faction scenes, define:

- what each faction wants
- what each faction cannot publicly accept
- what compromise costs
- which Specialty or training access is at stake
- who will remember the party's choice
