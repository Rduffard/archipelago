# Example Resolutions

These examples show how intent, Core Checks, Specialties, consequences, and faction pressure work together.

## Example 1: Forced Movement On A Storm Deck

Intent: hold a gangway while a boarding wave crashes across the deck.

Pressure: enemies may push the character overboard and split the crew.

Core Check: `Guard`.

Specialty: `Brace I` allows Guard instead of Reflex against forced movement.

Outcome:

- Success: the character holds the line; allies keep the retreat route.
- Success with cost: the character holds, but takes fatigue or loses a shield.
- Failure: the character is driven back; the enemy advance clock ticks.

Why it works: Guard remains the roll. Brace changes what defensive mastery permits.

## Example 2: Courtroom Exposure

Intent: expose a noble smuggling compact before the court adjourns.

Pressure: public accusation can backfire and damage reputation.

Possible checks:

- `Presence`: command the room.
- `Guile`: draw the noble into contradiction.
- `Broker`: introduce documents through accepted legal channels.
- `Composure`: endure counter-accusation.

Specialties:

- `Read` identifies the witness most ready to break.
- `Perform` turns the reveal into ceremony.

Consequences:

- Noble Courts may turn hostile.
- Rebel Movements may gain sympathy.
- Underworld Networks may mark the party as a liability.

Why it works: social conflict changes access, stance, and institutional response rather than simply persuading everyone.

## Example 3: Relic Bargain

Intent: ask a drowned shrine bell where the missing ship went.

Pressure: the bell answers through memory and corruption.

Possible checks:

- `Attunement`: feel the bell's emotional resonance.
- `Omen`: interpret the warning without forcing contact.
- `Ward`: protect the listener from spiritual backlash.
- `Invoke`: demand an answer directly.

Specialties:

- `Attune` may reveal the safest question.
- `Sift` may recover an echo without fully waking the relic.

Consequences:

- the party learns the route
- the bell remembers the speaker
- Faith Orders may detect the contact
- a corruption clock advances if the answer is forced

Why it works: arcane power gives useful information while deepening future pressure.

## Example 4: Checkpoint Without Papers

Intent: move a fugitive through an imperial checkpoint.

Pressure: the patrol can detain the fugitive, expose the crew, or record the ship.

Possible checks:

- `Broker`: produce a permit through known channels.
- `Signal`: spoof the clearance request.
- `Grace`: make the passage socially routine.
- `Pressure`: force the patrol to stand down.
- `Guile`: redirect suspicion toward another cargo hold.

Consequences:

- Yuma reputation may rise if the party cooperates.
- Rebel reputation may rise if the fugitive escapes.
- Underworld reputation may rise if bribery is used cleanly.
- the checkpoint alarm clock may advance on partial success.

Why it works: the same objective supports several playstyles, each with different faction meaning.
