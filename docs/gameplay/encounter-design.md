# Encounter Design

Encounters should test position, pressure, identity, and consequence rather than only damage throughput.

## Encounter Ingredients

Strong encounters include:

- a visible objective
- changing terrain or social pressure
- meaningful consequences
- opportunities for Specialty expression
- faction or reputation texture when appropriate

An encounter is any scene where pressure forces meaningful choice. It can be a duel, storm crossing, court accusation, relic negotiation, checkpoint, mutiny, salvage dive, or ritual containment.

## Encounter Frame

Before running an encounter, define:

- `Objective`: what the characters want.
- `Pressure`: what gets worse if they hesitate or fail.
- `Terrain`: physical, social, institutional, or spiritual constraints.
- `Witnesses`: who will remember what happens.
- `Specialty windows`: where mastery can change the route.
- `Exit states`: what the scene can become afterward.

This prevents encounters from becoming isolated rolls.

## Specialty Windows

Encounters should contain moments where Specialties matter:

- forced movement for `Brace`
- narrow lanes for `Suppress`
- false openings for `Feint`
- unstable machinery for `Jury-Rig`
- tense negotiation terms for `Bargain`
- haunted or relic-heavy pressure for `Attune`

Do not require a Specialty to solve the encounter. Let it create a memorable route through the problem.

## Encounter Pressure Types

- `Tempo`: enemies, storms, or politics move faster than the characters want.
- `Position`: the scene punishes bad placement or rewards preparation.
- `Access`: the objective sits behind permission, ritual, rank, or law.
- `Scarcity`: supplies, time, cover, or trust are limited.
- `Witness`: the consequence depends on who sees the action.
- `Corruption`: the easy solution invites spiritual cost.

## Example: Salvage Under Patrol

Objective: recover a sealed engine core from a wreck.

Pressure:

- Yuma patrol arrives in six clock segments.
- the wreck shifts each time the sea slams it.
- the core is spiritually warm and attracting attention.

Routes:

- `Patch` stabilizes flooded compartments.
- `Salvage` identifies the correct housing.
- `Signal` spoofs the patrol's lighthouse confirmation.
- `Attune` reads whether the core is safe to touch.
- `Suppress` or `Brace` matters if boarders arrive before extraction.

Exit states:

- recovered cleanly but spotted
- recovered hidden but corrupted
- abandoned but mapped for later
- traded to a faction for protection

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- encounters with only one valid check
- damage races with no positional or faction state
- Specialty gates where lack of a Specialty means no play
- consequence-free victories
- enemies who exist only as hit point containers
